Digital Marketing Campaign & Customer Acquisition Analytics
End-to-end marketing analytics project analyzing customer acquisition, e-commerce funnel performance, campaign efficiency, customer behavior, and revenue using BigQuery SQL, Excel, and Power BI.

## Executive Summary

This project demonstrates an end-to-end **Digital Marketing and Customer Acquisition Analytics** workflow for a D2C/e-commerce business.

The analysis combines **public GA4 e-commerce data** with a separately created **simulated advertising campaign dataset** to examine customer acquisition, website engagement, marketing-funnel performance, customer behavior, revenue, and advertising efficiency.

Using **BigQuery and SQL**, the data was extracted, transformed, aggregated, and validated into analytical datasets. **Excel** was then used for KPI validation, exploratory analysis, pivot-table analysis, funnel analysis, customer analysis, and campaign analysis. Finally, **Power BI and DAX** were used to create an interactive four-page marketing analytics dashboard.

The observed GA4 analysis identified key patterns in acquisition sources, funnel conversion, customer purchase behavior, repeat customers, and revenue contribution. The simulated advertising analysis demonstrated how metrics such as **CTR, CPC, conversion rate, CAC, and ROAS** can be used to evaluate campaign efficiency.

A key methodological principle of the project was maintaining a clear separation between **observed GA4 performance and simulated advertising performance**, avoiding unsupported campaign-level attribution.

The final outcome is a portfolio-ready analytics solution that demonstrates skills in **SQL, data transformation, Excel analysis, DAX, Power BI visualization, KPI development, data validation, funnel analysis, customer analytics, and business insight generation**.


## Business Problem

The business invests in multiple digital marketing channels to attract customers and generate online sales. However, marketing performance is not clearly understood across the customer journey.

The key challenge is to identify:

* Which acquisition sources bring the most users and revenue
* Where customers drop off in the e-commerce funnel
* How effectively users move from product views to purchases
* How one-time and repeat customers contribute to revenue
* Which simulated advertising campaigns generate stronger returns
* How marketing performance can be improved using data-driven insights

The objective of this project is to analyze the complete journey from **digital marketing and customer acquisition to website engagement, conversion, customers, and revenue**, and translate the findings into actionable business insights.

## Project Objectives

1. Analyze customer acquisition performance across different traffic sources and mediums.

2. Measure the e-commerce marketing funnel from **product views → add to cart → checkout → purchase**.

3. Identify major customer drop-off points and measure stage-level conversion rates.

4. Analyze customer behavior by distinguishing **non-purchasers, one-time customers, and repeat customers**.

5. Measure purchase frequency and understand its relationship with customer revenue.

6. Evaluate simulated advertising campaigns using key marketing metrics such as **CTR, CPC, CVR, CAC, and ROAS**.

7. Compare campaign performance across different advertising channels and campaign objectives.

8. Build an interactive Power BI dashboard to communicate marketing performance and business insights.

9. Validate analytical results across **BigQuery, Excel, and Power BI** to improve data accuracy and consistency.

10. Translate analytical findings into practical recommendations for improving **customer acquisition, conversion, and marketing efficiency**.


## Data & Methodology

### Data Sources

The project uses two complementary datasets:

**1. Public GA4 E-commerce Data — Observed Data**

A public Google Analytics 4 e-commerce sample dataset was used to analyze actual observed user behavior during the period **November 1, 2020 to January 31, 2021**.

The analysis includes:

* Users
* Sessions
* Page views
* Product views
* Add-to-cart events
* Checkout events
* Purchase events
* Revenue
* Acquisition source and medium
* Customer purchase behavior

**2. Simulated Advertising Campaign Data**

A separate simulated advertising dataset was created to demonstrate campaign-level marketing analysis where campaign spend, impressions, clicks, conversions, and campaign revenue were required.

The simulated dataset contains:

* Google Ads
* Meta Ads
* Display
* Six campaigns
* Impressions
* Clicks
* Ad spend
* Conversions
* Campaign revenue

All advertising metrics are explicitly identified as **SIMULATED**.

### Data Integrity Approach

Observed GA4 revenue and simulated advertising revenue were kept separate.

The simulated campaign revenue was **not combined with observed GA4 revenue**, because the public GA4 sample does not provide defensible campaign-level attribution for all simulated advertising channels.

This separation prevents misleading conclusions and clearly distinguishes **observed business data from simulated advertising assumptions**.

## Key Findings — Observed GA4 Performance

### 1. Customer acquisition generated significant traffic, but conversion remained limited

The observed dataset contains **269,989 unique users** and **5,692 purchase events** during the analysis period.

Using unique observed users as the denominator, the user-level conversion rate is approximately **2.11%**.

This indicates that a large proportion of observed users did not progress to a purchase.

### 2. The largest funnel drop occurs between product engagement and purchase

The observed funnel contains:

* **386,068 Product Views**
* **58,543 Add-to-Carts**
* **38,757 Checkouts**
* **5,692 Purchases**

The stage conversion rates are:

* Product View → Add to Cart: **15.16%**
* Add to Cart → Checkout: **66.20%**
* Checkout → Purchase: **14.69%**

The relatively low checkout-to-purchase rate indicates that checkout completion is an important area for further investigation.

### 3. Google organic generated the largest observed revenue contribution

`google / organic` generated approximately **$95,775** in observed revenue and represented about **26.45% of observed revenue** in the acquisition-source analysis.

It also accounted for approximately **31.04% of the source-level user volume**.

This shows that organic search was a major traffic and revenue acquisition source in the observed dataset.

### 4. Referral traffic showed strong revenue contribution relative to its user share

`shop.googlemerchandisestore.com / referral` represented approximately **7.82% of source-level users** but contributed approximately **12.85% of observed revenue**.

Its observed revenue per source user was approximately **$1.78**, compared with approximately **$1.09** across the source-level analysis.

This suggests that this referral source generated relatively higher-value traffic.

### 5. Repeat customers represented a smaller customer group but contributed substantial revenue

There were **775 repeat customers**, representing approximately **17.54% of purchasing customers**.

However, repeat customers generated approximately **$128,042**, or about **35.35% of observed purchaser revenue**.

This demonstrates that repeat customers generated a disproportionately large share of revenue relative to their customer count.

### 6. Revenue increased with purchase frequency

Customers with higher purchase-event frequency generated higher revenue per customer.

Average revenue per user within the purchasing-frequency groups increased from approximately:

* **1 purchase:** $64.25
* **2 purchases:** $138.35
* **3 purchases:** $181.86
* **4+ purchases:** $298.70

This indicates a strong relationship between purchase frequency and customer revenue within the observed dataset.

### 7. Observed monthly performance varied considerably

Observed revenue was approximately:

* **November 2020:** $144,260
* **December 2020:** $160,555
* **January 2021:** $57,350

December recorded the highest observed revenue in the analysis period, while January showed a substantial decline.

Monthly performance should therefore be considered when evaluating changes in traffic, engagement, and purchasing behavior.


## Key Findings — Simulated Advertising Performance

The following findings are based on the **simulated advertising dataset** and should not be interpreted as observed GA4 campaign attribution.

### 1. Simulated campaign efficiency varied considerably

Across the six simulated campaigns, ROAS ranged from approximately **0.71 to 2.47**.

This demonstrates that campaign performance can vary substantially even within the same advertising program.

### 2. Brand Search recorded the highest simulated ROAS

The simulated Brand Search campaign generated:

* Spend: **$274,757.51**
* Conversions: **9,991**
* CAC: **$27.50**
* Simulated campaign revenue: **$679,412.48**
* ROAS: **2.47**

These figures are simulated campaign outputs rather than observed GA4 attribution.

### 3. Prospecting had the highest simulated CAC

The simulated Meta Ads Prospecting campaign generated:

* Spend: **$52,068.01**
* Conversions: **596**
* CAC: **$87.36**
* ROAS: **0.71**

This indicates that the simulated campaign generated fewer conversions relative to its advertising spend than the other simulated campaigns.

### 4. Non-Brand Search produced substantial traffic but lower simulated return

The simulated Non-Brand Search campaign generated:

* **99,179 clicks**
* **2,184 conversions**
* **$163,642.40 spend**
* **$157,099.53 simulated revenue**
* **0.96 ROAS**

The campaign generated considerable traffic, but simulated revenue was below simulated advertising spend.

### 5. Google Ads represented the largest simulated advertising investment

Across the simulated dataset:

* Google Ads spend: **$629,315.96**
* Meta Ads spend: **$118,186.14**
* Display spend: **$24,794.33**

Google Ads therefore represented the majority of simulated advertising spend in this dataset.

### 6. Simulated campaign performance should not be combined with observed GA4 revenue

The campaign dataset was intentionally kept separate from observed GA4 revenue.

Google Ads has a corresponding `google / cpc` acquisition source in the observed dataset, but the public GA4 sample does not provide sufficient campaign-level attribution to reliably connect each simulated campaign to observed purchases and revenue.

Therefore, the analysis reports:

**Observed GA4 performance** and **simulated advertising performance** separately.

This preserves analytical transparency and avoids presenting simulated attribution as measured business performance.


## Business Recommendations

The recommendations below are derived from the observed GA4 analysis and the separately identified simulated advertising analysis.

### 1. Investigate checkout abandonment

The observed **Checkout → Purchase rate was 14.69%**, indicating substantial drop-off during the final conversion stage.

The business should investigate potential causes such as:

* Checkout usability
* Payment failures
* Unexpected costs
* Shipping information requirements
* Mobile checkout experience
* Page-load performance
* Trust and security concerns

A funnel-focused optimization program could then be used to test improvements.

### 2. Strengthen product-page-to-cart conversion

The observed **Product View → Add-to-Cart rate was 15.16%**.

The business could investigate product-page elements such as:

* Product descriptions
* Images and videos
* Pricing presentation
* Product availability
* Calls to action
* Reviews and social proof

A/B testing can be used to determine which changes improve add-to-cart behavior.

### 3. Investigate high-value acquisition sources

Some acquisition sources generated a larger share of revenue relative to their share of users.

For example, `shop.googlemerchandisestore.com / referral` generated approximately **12.85% of observed revenue** while representing approximately **7.82% of source-level users**.

The business could investigate what characteristics make traffic from this source more valuable and whether similar referral opportunities exist.

### 4. Develop a repeat-customer strategy

Repeat customers represented approximately **17.54% of purchasing customers** but contributed approximately **35.35% of purchaser revenue**.

Potential initiatives include:

* Personalized follow-up campaigns
* Product recommendations
* Loyalty programs
* Cross-selling
* Post-purchase engagement
* Re-engagement campaigns

The objective would be to encourage additional purchases while monitoring incremental revenue and customer retention.

### 5. Evaluate advertising campaigns using efficiency metrics

The simulated campaign analysis demonstrates the importance of evaluating campaigns using multiple metrics rather than clicks alone.

Campaign evaluation should consider:

* CTR
* CPC
* Conversion Rate
* CAC
* ROAS
* Conversion volume
* Revenue contribution

In an actual business environment, budget decisions should be based on validated campaign-attribution data rather than the simulated results used in this portfolio project.

### 6. Investigate seasonal and monthly performance changes

Observed revenue changed substantially across the analysis period, from approximately **$144,260 in November** to **$160,555 in December**, followed by **$57,350 in January**.

The business should investigate whether these changes are associated with:

* Seasonal demand
* Promotional activity
* Changes in traffic quality
* Product availability
* Marketing activity
* Customer purchasing behavior

### 7. Establish an ongoing marketing performance dashboard

The Power BI dashboard can be used as a framework for ongoing monitoring.

A production version could track:

* Acquisition
* Funnel conversion
* Customer retention
* Revenue
* Campaign spend
* CAC
* ROAS
* Channel performance

This would allow marketing teams to monitor performance continuously and identify changes requiring further investigation.

## Limitations & Assumptions

### 1. Public sample data

The observed e-commerce data comes from a public GA4 sample dataset and does not represent a specific real company's complete production environment.

Therefore, findings should be interpreted as analytical observations from the sample rather than conclusions about a real business.

### 2. Simulated advertising data

Advertising spend, campaign conversions, and campaign revenue were simulated for portfolio-analysis purposes.

These values should not be presented as actual advertising performance.

### 3. Campaign attribution limitation

The public GA4 sample does not provide sufficient campaign-level attribution to reliably connect all simulated advertising campaigns with observed purchases and revenue.

Therefore, observed GA4 revenue and simulated campaign revenue were intentionally kept separate.

### 4. User aggregation

The user-level funnel table represents users across the complete analysis period.

Therefore, it is not appropriate to treat the user-level table as a daily time-series dataset.

Date-based analysis was therefore performed using the dedicated date-level performance table where applicable.

### 5. Purchase-event interpretation

Purchase frequency is based on recorded purchase events in the dataset.

It should not automatically be interpreted as the number of unique orders without additional transaction-level validation.

### 6. Source-level user counts

Users aggregated by acquisition source and medium may be counted in multiple source/medium groups.

Therefore, source-level user totals should not be interpreted as a unique-user total for the entire dataset.

### 7. Portfolio purpose

The project is designed to demonstrate an end-to-end marketing analytics workflow involving data extraction, transformation, validation, visualization, and business interpretation.

It is not intended to reproduce the internal reporting environment of a specific organization.
